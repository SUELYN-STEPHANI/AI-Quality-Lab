## 🎯 Program Overview

Advanced professional development focused on the analysis and interpretation
of meaning in Brazilian Portuguese.

The program explores how meaning is constructed through words, sentences,
context, discourse, intention and cultural knowledge.

The objective is to develop the ability to distinguish between:

**what is written · what is meant · what is implied · what can be inferred**

These competencies are directly applicable to linguistic quality assurance
and Large Language Model evaluation.

## 03 — Denotation & Connotation

Analysis of literal and associated meanings.

The evaluator distinguishes between:

**Denotation** — direct or conventional meaning

**Connotation** — contextual, emotional or culturally associated meaning

- Advertising
- Informal communication
- Literary language
- Persuasive language
- AI-generated content

## 05 — Contextual Meaning

Evaluation of how context modifies interpretation.

Analysis considers:

- Previous sentences
- Conversation history
- Speaker
- Audience
- Situation

- Shared knowledge

## 06 — Textual Interpretation

Structured analysis of written content.

Competencies include:

- Identifying the central idea

- Recognizing arguments
- Identifying conclusions
- Distinguishing fact from opinion
- Identifying explicit information
- Identifying implicit information

- Recognizing author intention

---

## 07 — Explicit vs. Implicit Meaning

Training in distinguishing information directly stated from information
that must be inferred.

### Explicit Meaning

Information directly available in the text.

### Implicit Meaning

Information communicated indirectly through context, structure or shared
knowledge.

This distinction is critical in AI evaluation because models may transform
an inference into an unsupported factual statement.

## 24 — Semantic Consistency

- Meaning shifts
- Entity confusion
- Inconsistent terminology

---

## 25 — Textual Interpretation Applied to AI

Application of semantic and interpretative analysis to LLM-generated content.

Evaluation includes:

- User intent understanding
- Meaning preservation
- Context retention
- Correct inference
- Unsupported inference
- Ambiguity
- Semantic distortion
- Contradiction
- Response relevance
- Implicit information
- Instruction interpretation

---

## 26 — Semantic Hallucination Detection

Identification of cases in which an AI response introduces meaning that is
not supported by the source or prompt.

Examples include:

- Invented conclusions
- Unsupported causal relationships
- Incorrect inference
- Added assumptions
- Misinterpreted context
- Excessive generalization

A response may contain no obvious factual fabrication and still present
a **semantic hallucination**.
